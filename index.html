Before you start - safety first

HSE
Damaged building: do not enter until local authorities say it is safe. Leave straight away if you hear shifting or unusual noises. If a building inspector has put a colour-coded sign on the home, do not enter until you get more information from local authorities. (CDC, Red Cross)
Power lines: stay away from fallen, low or damaged power lines and report them to the electricity company or emergency services. (CDC, Queensland Electrical Safety Office)
Gas smell or hissing: get everyone out to fresh air, no naked flames or electrical switches, and call your gas emergency number from a safe place. (Gas Safe Register, Red Cross)
  • Stay off the roof and off ladders. Treat all roofs as fragile. Look from the ground. (UK HSE Roof work)
  • Keep away from exposed wiring; it may still be live. Do not use mains-powered appliances until a registered electrician says it is safe. (Electrical Safety First)
  • Floodwater can carry germs and chemicals. Wear boots and gloves, and wash your hands with soap and water after contact. (CDC, London Fire Brigade)

Safe steps from public guidance, in the order they matter. None of them involve climbing, structural work, electrical or gas work. In a property you do not own, share these with the owner or landlord.

    What this tool will not do

    LIMITS
    • It does not decide whether a building is safe to enter. Local authorities, a building inspector or a structural engineer do that.
    • It does not assess an insurance claim or say what your policy covers. Your insurer or its assessor does that.
    • It never asks you to climb, enter floodwater, touch wet electrics, work on gas or fuel equipment, or cut fallen trees.

    Standards & Measurements

    REFERENCES

    Guidance used in this tool

    • Entering: do not enter a damaged building until local authorities determine it is safe; leave if you hear shifting or unusual noises. Check outside first for loose power lines, damaged gas lines, foundation cracks and missing supports; avoid sagging ceilings and floors. (CDC, Red Cross)
    • Electrics: switch off at the consumer unit only if it is safe; keep away from exposed wiring; a registered electrician should check before mains appliances are used. Water-affected hard-wired appliances (for example air-conditioning units, stoves) checked by a licensed electrician before re-use. (Electrical Safety First, Queensland Electrical Safety Office)
    • Generators: never indoors, in a basement or garage; outside and at least 6 m (20 ft) from any window, door or vent. Connect to house wiring only through a changeover switch installed by a licensed electrician, never through a power point (back-feeding). (CDC, Queensland Electrical Safety Office)
    • Utilities: turn off the water main if pipes are damaged; avoid sinks, showers and toilets if sewage lines may be damaged; disconnect and check appliances before use. (Red Cross)
    • Insurance evidence: photograph or video the damage, then take reasonable steps to protect the property; keep receipts; keep damaged items until the adjuster inspects them, or photograph them first if they must go. (Insurance Information Institute, London Fire Brigade)
    • Drying and mould: dry water-damaged areas and items within 24-48 hours; mould areas under about 0.9 m² (10 sq ft, roughly 1 m by 1 m (3 ft by 3 ft)) can usually be cleaned yourself. Drying out can take weeks or months. (EPA Mold & Moisture, London Fire Brigade)
    • Protective gear: rubber boots, gloves, protective clothing, goggles or a face mask after floodwater; soft furnishings hot-washed at 60 °C (140 °F) or above. (London Fire Brigade)
    • Units: 1 m = 3.28 ft; 1 cm = 0.39 in; 0.9 m² = 10 sq ft. Metric first, imperial in brackets.
    Country-specific: emergency numbers, gas emergency lines, electricity company contacts, building inspection systems (such as colour-coded placards) and insurance claim rules differ by country and region. Follow your local authorities' instructions first, and check your own policy wording.

    Items marked Professional practice reflect general inspection and HSE practice. No single public standard covers them, so no standard is claimed.

      Before you start: how your information is saved

      Your information is saved on this device only.

      Save a backup file regularly.

      Where is my information saved?

      Inside this browser, on this device. Nothing is sent to The Snag Tag or anyone else. That keeps it private, but it also means you are the only one who can keep a copy safe.

      Why could my information seem to disappear?
      • You open the tool in a different browser, a private/incognito window, or on another device.
      • You open a different copy of the tool (for example a downloaded file instead of your link).
      • Your browsing history or site data gets cleared.
      • Safari (iPhone, iPad and Mac) can delete saved information if you don't open the tool for about 7 days.
      • The tool is open in two tabs at the same time. Always use one tab.
      How do I keep my information safe?
      • Press Save a backup now after every session where you change something.
      • Keep the backup file somewhere safe, like your cloud drive or a USB stick.
      • Always open the tool from the same link, in the same browser.
      How do I get my information back, or use another device?

      Open the tool, press Restore a backup and choose your latest backup file. Everything comes back as it was when you saved it.
